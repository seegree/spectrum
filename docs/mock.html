Direction: tiles and number together

The dropdown is gone. Order is now one radio group of nine tiles — the same selection model as everything else (§1) — and the angle field sits beside it as a readout you can also type into:

  • Click → and the field shows 0. Click ↑ and it shows 90. The tile teaches you what the number means, which is exactly the “what does zero look like?” problem.
  • Type 37 and the panel goes spatial at 37° with no tile lit — the eight tiles are shortcuts, not a cage. Precision survives.
  • ≡ selects timeline stacking order and disables the field, because there is no angle in that mode.
  • The arrow points the way the ramp travels: first stop at the tail, last stop at the head.

This also killed a control: no Order dropdown, no Space dropdown, and row 3 is narrower than the stop row.

Dragging swatches — possible, but expensive

Straight answer: yes, but it costs the thing that makes the swatches reliable. ScriptUI has no drag-and-drop. Dragging between swatches means one onDraw control that paints all eight and does its own hit testing on mousedown/mousemove/mouseup, because separate controls cannot track a drag across each other.

The swatches are native-background groups today for one reason: they keep painting while the modal color picker is open. Collapsing them into one custom-painted control trades that away — the whole stop row would blank behind the picker, which is the control you are most often using.

And with even spacing, dragging can only reorder colors, never reposition them — so ⇄ already covers the common case, and re-picking a swatch covers the rest. Recommend v0.2, bundled with draggable stop positions, where the same onDraw machinery pays for both at once.

There is no locked-layer case — corrected

An earlier draft of this panel had a “locked layers: skip / unlock” setting and counted locked layers in the skip report. That case cannot occur. Locking a layer in After Effects makes it unselectable, so comp.selectedLayers never contains one — a panel that works from the selection can never meet a locked layer.

Recorded here so it does not get re-invented: the setting is gone, the writer has no layer.locked branch, and the skip report has no locked bucket. The real skip causes are all property-level, not layer-level — keyframed colors, expression-driven colors, gradient fills, and layers with no paint operators at all.

Near-complementary pairs — known behavior

Hue interpolation takes the shortest way around the wheel. When two colors sit near opposite each other, which way is shorter becomes knife-edge: #1B2A6B → #F2C14E measures 176.4° of arc, and a few hex values of change in the blue takes it to 178.4°. Cross 180° and the whole ramp reroutes the other way around the wheel — through teal instead of magenta — from an edit you cannot see. Hit Near-complement, then nudge a swatch.

Hence the default is navy → aqua at ~71° of arc, nowhere near the edge. And it is the strongest practical argument for the middle stop: a third stop pins the route explicitly rather than leaving it to a coin flip. No hue-direction control needed.

Try it

  • Click a swatch to pick a color; Option/Alt-click to remove that one. Hover anything for its tooltip.
  • + adds a stop sampled from the current ramp — the strip does not jump. − takes the last interior stop back off.
  • Click ↗ and watch the angle field; then type 37 and watch the tiles let go.
  • Click the eyedropper — it samples colors off the selected layers and reports which layer each came from.
  • Open ⚙ and switch to RGB on a near-complementary ramp to see the gray middle OKLCH avoids.
